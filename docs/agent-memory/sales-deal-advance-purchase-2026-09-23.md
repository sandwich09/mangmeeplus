A durable `PAID` operation and pending invoice preserve the original package until its end, then activate the deal once and schedule its next collection at the end of the prepaid deal cycle. A zero-value subscription trial invoice cannot count as first-payment proof.
- Status and the billing page show the old active package plus the paid waiting deal with start/end/next collection dates. A future invoice cannot replace the old access invoice prematurely. Card and QR renewal subscriptions are checked for sibling collectors and early positive invoices. If a paid item is HELD or activation is BLOCKED, reconciliation stops any bound future subscription to prevent another collection while it is reviewed. A refund/dispute before start also cancels the future subscription and releases the purchase reservation after observing cancellation.
- Stripe documents a maximum two-year `trial_end` from the billing anchor. For unusually long stacked one-time terms, the already-paid operation remains reserved and the worker binds the future subscription when its end enters that window, with a two-day margin. The worker schedules that next check at the binding date instead of polling Stripe every five minutes; signed payment/refund events can still invoke reconciliation sooner. It cannot create a second purchase in the meantime. [Stripe subscription create API](https://docs.stripe.com/api/subscriptions/create).

Regular trialing card subscriptions remain excluded.

Exhaustion messaging and admin meter/readiness were audited by the delegated agent. The proposed pay-per-credit top-up and a sales deal price floor remain outside this change by owner direction.

## Source audit 2026-09-23

- The bug is reproducible from source: `BillingService.status()` offers `CARD_AUTO` and `PROMPTPAY_RENEW` via `dealPurchaseModes()` for an active one-time account, but `quote()` rejects those modes with `BILLING_MODE_CHANGE_PENDING` at lines 307–311. Existing `billing-purchase-source.pg.spec.ts` explicitly asserts that refusal for catalog purchases; keep that behavior for non-deal transitions.
- The normal `CARD_AUTO` Checkout uses `mode=subscription`, which would immediately start a paid cycle. `PROMPTPAY_RENEW` uses `mode=payment`, but its quote has a separate live-account guard. The reconciler routes only `CARD_ONCE`, `PROMPTPAY_ONCE`, and `PROMPTPAY_RENEW` to the payment Checkout reader. The one-time apply immediately changes term, account, menu, AI deal status, and receipt grants, so none of these branches currently fulfills §19.10.
- `pendingPlanChangeId` only recognizes scheduled downgrades/prepaid one-time changes in `pendingChange()`; `status()` passes every pending ID to `BillingCollection.get()`. A new deferred deal operation needs its own status projection, activation handler, and retry path rather than storing an unrecognized operation in the existing field.
- `status()` picks the invoice with latest `periodEnd`; a future prepaid invoice could become the status invoice before the old term ends. Keep current access/payment projection on the active term and expose the deferred deal separately. The return attempt should show `PAID` and `PENDING` until activation.
- Stripe's official Checkout API permits `payment_intent_data.setup_future_usage=off_session` with an existing Customer, which attaches the successful card for reuse. The later `charge_automatically` subscription still needs explicit verified `default_payment_method` and an end-of-first-deal-cycle `trial_end`; QR should use the existing `send_invoice` renewal pattern. Verify the zero invoice and all sibling subscriptions before acknowledging binding. Sources: https://docs.stripe.com/api/checkout/sessions/create and https://docs.stripe.com/api/subscriptions/create.
- Refund/dispute before activation must invalidate the queued opening and cancel the future subscription without touching the old term. No current refund flow makes that connection. Provider events for the newly created subscription must also be recognized as deferred, so they cannot reconcile it as the current account subscription early.

## Commercial review 2026-09-23

- §19.10's delayed activation preserves the old paid obligation, takes cash for the first future cycle now, and avoids giving early premium limits, menus, AI credit, or receipt units for free. The economic contribution belongs to the future service cycle; early cash alone is not incremental profit.
- Compare a proposed deal with the actual counterfactual: expected contribution from renewing at catalog price or lapsing, not with zero revenue by default. For one cycle, contribution = customer price excluding any VAT − Stripe/payment/invoice fees − incremental OCR/AI/storage/support cost − sales/credit/refund reserve. A deal is commercially preferable when its expected contribution plus retention value exceeds the no-deal path.
- The current sales-deal rules accept prices down to 1 satang, with no enforced cost or contribution floor (`sales-deal.logic.ts` R3/D1). An operator-first policy would require a hard floor based on the *custom limits and features in the deal*, with an audited Owner-only override, and normally a finite `cycles` value for discounted auto-renewal. This changes the current approved D1 policy; do not silently alter it as part of the billing implementation.
- §19.3's existing one-time deal grants deal rights immediately while extending the term from old `paidThrough`; that can give premium rights or OCR quota during the already-paid old period. This commercial behavior differs from §19.10's deferred rights and merits a separate product decision. Existing CARD_AUTO deal conversion also applies immediately without proration, so the customer may forfeit residual paid time.
- §17.2 counts sales attribution only once per company on first paid activation; selling a discounted renewal to an existing shop does not itself create a new attribution row. If renewal incentives are desired, define them separately against realized contribution after activation and refund/dispute review.
